# Personal Brand Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the working personal-brand system represented by the
supplied THX logo. It is designed to keep website copy, portfolio writing,
social content, presentations, profile bios, and visual decisions consistent.

The logo is the confirmed reference. Audience, role, offer, achievements,
client names, metrics, and testimonials were not provided, so bracketed items
and positioning statements below are working drafts rather than claims.

## Brand Snapshot

### Brand mark

The supplied mark appears to be a stylized **THX** monogram built from sharp,
geometric letterforms. It combines:

- polished silver and white 3D surfaces
- deep graphite and near-black depth
- electric cyan edge lighting
- saturated teal and turquoise highlights
- angular, forward-pointing shapes
- a wide horizontal silhouette that remains recognizable at a glance

The mark communicates technical confidence, precision, movement, and modern
craft. Use it as the visual anchor for the identity.

### Brand idea

Make complex work feel clear, deliberate, and ready to move forward.

### Working personality

The brand should feel:

- sharp, not aggressive
- confident, not boastful
- technical, not cold
- premium, not flashy
- direct, not blunt
- modern, not trend-dependent
- human, not overly polished

### Working positioning statement

Use this as a starting point until the audience and offer are confirmed:

> I help [audience] turn [complex problem or opportunity] into [clear,
> useful outcome] through focused thinking, thoughtful design, and disciplined
> execution.

Do not publish the bracketed version. Replace it with a specific audience,
problem, and outcome before using it as final positioning.

## Messaging Foundation

### Primary promise

Clarity that helps people understand the work, trust the thinking, and take
the next step.

### Supporting themes

1. **Clarity**: Make the important idea easy to understand.
2. **Craft**: Show care in the details that affect the result.
3. **Momentum**: Turn insight into practical forward movement.
4. **Trust**: Be precise about what is known, proven, proposed, and still
   being tested.

### Message hierarchy

When the format allows, develop messages in this order:

1. the reader's situation or desired outcome
2. the useful benefit
3. the approach or mechanism
4. evidence that makes the claim credible
5. a clear next action

Do not lead with tools, process, credentials, or implementation details unless
they are directly relevant to the reader's decision.

### Audience template

Complete these fields before writing a major page or campaign:

```text
Primary audience: [one specific reader or buyer]
They are trying to: [desired outcome]
They are frustrated by: [problem, risk, or friction]
I help by: [specific offer, capability, or approach]
The evidence available is: [work, result, artifact, experience, or proof]
The next action is: [specific action and outcome]
```

If several audiences matter, give each audience its own message rather than
using vague language that tries to speak to everyone at once.

## Voice and Tone

### Voice

Write with:

- plain language
- concrete nouns and active verbs
- calm authority
- useful specificity
- concise explanations
- measured confidence
- a human point of view

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website hero | Clear and assured | Outcome, relevance, next step |
| Portfolio case study | Analytical and concrete | Problem, decisions, result, evidence |
| About page | Warm and thoughtful | Point of view, working style, motivation |
| Social post | Direct and observant | Useful idea, tension, or lesson |
| Proposal | Precise and reassuring | Scope, fit, method, risk reduction |
| Technical explanation | Simple but substantive | Mechanism, tradeoffs, implications |
| Call to action | Specific and low-friction | Verb plus reader outcome |

### Sentence behavior

Prefer a mix of short and medium-length sentences. Use a short sentence for
emphasis only when it earns the emphasis. Do not create artificial rhythm by
turning every sentence into a dramatic fragment.

Use contractions when they make the voice more natural. Preserve intentional
informality when it fits the platform. Avoid em dashes as a default styling
habit.

### Words to favor

Use words that make the work feel tangible:

```text
clear, focused, deliberate, useful, practical, precise, thoughtful,
credible, resilient, considered, build, shape, simplify, explain, test,
ship, improve, connect, clarify, solve, decide, explore
```

### Words and patterns to avoid

Avoid language that sounds generic, inflated, or machine-written:

```text
revolutionary, world-class, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-level,
unlock your potential, transform your business, in today's fast-paced world,
passionate about, seamless, holistic, innovative solutions, click here
```

These words are not forbidden when they are genuinely accurate and specific,
but they should never replace evidence or a clear explanation.

## Recommended Tagline Directions

These are candidate lines, not established claims. Choose one only after
confirming that it reflects the actual work and audience.

- **Build with clarity. Move with intent.**
- **Make the complex useful.**
- **Clear thinking. Deliberate execution.**
- **Ideas shaped for forward motion.**
- **Precision that moves work forward.**

The strongest direction for this visual identity is concise, action-oriented,
and grounded in clarity rather than hype.

## Visual Identity

### Working color palette

The values below are practical working tokens estimated from the supplied
mark. Treat the logo artwork as the source of truth and adjust these values
if a production color specification becomes available.

| Token | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Void | `#05080A` | Primary dark background |
| Graphite | `#111A21` | Panels, depth, dark surfaces |
| Steel | `#AAB8C2` | Secondary text and cool neutral detail |
| Silver | `#E8EEF2` | Light surfaces and primary neutral accents |
| Ice | `#F8FAFC` | Primary light text and high-contrast backgrounds |
| Electric Cyan | `#00E5FF` | Edge light, links, active states, highlights |
| Signal Teal | `#00D6B4` | Primary accent, success, calls to action |
| Deep Teal | `#006B70` | Accent shadow, gradients, secondary emphasis |

### Color ratios

Use the palette with restraint:

- 60% Void or Graphite
- 25% Ice, Silver, or Steel
- 10% Signal Teal
- 5% Electric Cyan for sharp highlights

The cyan and teal accents should guide attention, not fill every surface. A
dark field with one deliberate luminous accent will feel more premium than a
page saturated with neon color.

### Backgrounds

Preferred backgrounds:

- near-black with subtle graphite depth
- solid graphite for cards and panels
- light Ice or Silver when a clean editorial contrast is needed
- restrained dark-to-teal gradients for hero or feature moments

Avoid busy backgrounds behind the mark or behind important copy. Keep enough
contrast for the silver letterforms and cyan edge lighting to remain legible.

### Typography direction

Use a geometric or humanist grotesk that supports the logo's structured
character without copying its angular letterforms too literally.

Recommended starting system:

- **Headings**: Space Grotesk, Semibold or Bold
- **Body**: Inter, Regular or Medium
- **Labels and technical metadata**: JetBrains Mono, sparingly

Fallbacks should be `Arial`, `Helvetica`, or another clean system sans-serif.
Keep body text comfortable and readable. The logo can be dramatic; the text
should make the message easier to understand.

### Shape and layout language

Carry the mark's geometry into supporting design without decorating every
section:

- use strong horizontal alignment
- use angled cuts or chevrons as occasional accents
- use clear grids and intentional spacing
- use thin cyan or teal rules for separation
- prefer crisp corners or a restrained radius system
- use depth through contrast before using shadows

Do not turn the interface into a collection of arrows. The forward-motion
motif is strongest when used selectively.

### Imagery

Choose imagery with:

- high contrast and controlled lighting
- cool neutrals with selective teal or cyan light
- real work, tools, materials, interfaces, or environments
- strong composition and visible intent
- enough negative space for copy when text overlays are needed

Avoid generic smiling-team stock photography, excessive cyberpunk effects,
random neon backgrounds, and imagery that promises a lifestyle unrelated to
the work.

## Logo Use

### Preferred use

- Place the full mark on Void or Graphite backgrounds.
- Preserve the silver and cyan/teal contrast.
- Give the mark generous clear space.
- Use the full-color mark for primary identity moments.
- Use a single-color version only when production constraints require it.

### Clear space

As a working rule, leave clear space around the mark equal to at least the
height of its top horizontal bar on all sides. Increase the space in premium
or editorial layouts.

### Do not

- stretch, compress, rotate, or crop the mark
- place it over a busy image
- add an unrelated glow, bevel, or drop shadow
- recolor the silver as a random gradient
- use cyan and teal at full intensity everywhere around it
- place dark text directly over the dark parts of the mark
- redraw the letterforms from memory
- use a low-resolution screenshot when the original artwork is available

## Content Applications

### Website hero

The first viewport should answer:

1. What do you do?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What should the reader do next?

Working structure:

```text
Eyebrow: [specific category or audience]
Headline: [clear outcome or meaningful tension]
Subheadline: [how you create that outcome, in plain language]
Primary CTA: [verb + outcome]
Secondary proof: [specific work, artifact, or credible signal]
```

Do not use the logo or a dramatic introduction as a substitute for a clear
value proposition.

### Portfolio project

Use this order when it fits the project:

1. the problem or opportunity
2. the role and constraints
3. the important decisions
4. the work or mechanism
5. the result that can be supported
6. what was learned or improved

Keep technical details when they help the reader understand judgment,
tradeoffs, or credibility. Do not add metrics that the project cannot prove.

### About section

Lead with the kind of work and thinking the reader can expect. Avoid opening
with a generic claim about being passionate, driven, or a lifelong learner.

Working template:

> I work at the intersection of [discipline 1] and [discipline 2], helping
> [audience] make [type of work] clearer, more useful, and easier to move
> forward.

### Social content

Start with one useful idea, observation, lesson, or tension. Then explain the
reasoning and connect it to a practical implication.

Avoid empty engagement prompts, manufactured controversy, and vague
motivation. The reader should receive the useful idea before being asked to
respond.

## Rules for Writing on Behalf of This Brand

When creating copy for this brand:

1. Identify the reader and desired outcome before choosing the wording.
2. Lead with reader value, not a list of tools or credentials.
3. Keep the writing specific enough that it could not belong to any company.
4. Make claims only as strong as the available evidence.
5. Put proof close to the claim it supports.
6. Preserve a direct, thoughtful, technically literate voice.
7. Use short sections and meaningful subheads for scanability.
8. Prefer a clear verb and outcome in calls to action.
9. Preserve personality instead of replacing it with corporate polish.
10. Return finished copy when finished copy is requested.

Before finalizing, check for generic openings, vague headlines, unsupported
claims, unnecessary information, fake proof, repetitive dramatic structures,
and a mismatch between the hero promise and the body content.

## Brand Guardrails

Never invent or imply:

- customer or client names
- revenue, growth, conversion, or performance numbers
- awards, partnerships, or certifications
- seniority, scale, or production impact
- testimonials or endorsements
- capabilities that have not been confirmed

If information is missing, use a placeholder, ask for the fact when it is
necessary, or write the strongest narrower version that remains true.
